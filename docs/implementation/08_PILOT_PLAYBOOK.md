# Pedro Clisante Field Pilot: Start-to-Launch Playbook
## Deployment intention
A *permissioned* pilot on a precisely defined candidate 50–200m corridor on/near Pedro Clisante in Sosúa, not an assertion that a specific pole, camera or owner has approved access. Begin with one camera viewpoint; optionally add a second only when it closes a documented blind spot. Publish exact location only after approved surveying.

## Pilot objectives
**P1 — Measurement:** Count pedestrian crossings, moto/scooter, bicycle and car flows by direction and hour with known confidence and downtime.
**P2 — Utility:** Deliver a Business Pulse report to multiple voluntary local businesses; document specific operational decisions.
**P3 — Communication:** Produce a 30–60m NPC-hosted weekly Block Watch Live show using safe/redacted/permissioned views or data-only visualization.
**P4 — Revenue:** Operate one or two approved sponsor campaigns; validate creative production effort and REVELV delivery logs.
**P5 — Civic:** Publish a methodological public mobility summary and invite an engineer/community review of an actionable improvement question.

## Field sequence
### Week group A — Discovery and legal/site validation
1. Interview local business owners, tourism/event organizers and authority representatives.
2. Rank 3 possible observation zones for activity, power/network, permissions, coverage and public acceptance.
3. Acquire written installation and filming authorization; confirm signage, mounting, building owner and municipal/heritage issues.
4. Map excluded windows, residential entrances and sensitive areas; define public view rules.
5. Create bilingual notice and accessible community contact link.
**Do not capture public footage until gate G1 is met.**

### Week group B — Offline prototype
1. Seed fictional day/night movement data in the staging environment.
2. Create 1-minute → 15-minute/hour rollup reports with explicit data-quality flags.
3. Produce 3 preliminary designs: operator, business, viewer.
4. Test phone-based permissions/notification experience; configure simulated triggers.
5. Prepare safe HAI NPC script library; no claims about real people.
6. Capture data-collector test samples only under permitted conditions.

### Week group C — Controlled field measurement
1. Install hardware safely through authorized personnel with documented power/camera settings.
2. Set clock/NTP, zone lines, orientation, masks, encrypted transport and monitoring.
3. Run manual ground-truth counts in multiple conditions and compare.
4. Inspect classes with lower accuracy; revise calibration or narrow coverage.
5. Maintain hourly validity report and device uptime.
6. Adjust for events/rain/darkness; lock method version when accepted.

### Week group D — Operational proofs
1. Business Pulse trial: daypart + weekday/weekend comparison; discuss staffing/event/promo hypothesis with merchants.
2. Produce public “State of the Block” summary with dataset coverage and limitations.
3. Four weekly safe media episodes; include one contest using predefined reliable counts.
4. Run sponsor creative with disclosure, approved placement, frequency cap and event reconciliation.
5. Publish internal pilot economics and civic partner insight brief.

### Week group E — Independent evaluation
1. Reconcile labels, rejected windows and false positives.
2. Compare actual staff hours/creative costs against revenue.
3. Measure business adoption, merchant responses, viewer returning participation.
4. Record legal/privacy feedback and mitigation.
5. Choose GATE DECISION: SCALE, ITERATE, PAUSE, or STOP.
6. If scaling, publish a second-camera or second-street procurement justification.

## Pilot sample reports (template)
**Business Pulse:** reporting period, valid observed minutes, count by 15m and daypart, median of comparable weekdays, direction, weather/event notes, suggested action (clearly a hypothesis), follow-up planned.
**Event Impact:** event calendar and geofence, baseline matched windows, before/during/after, uncertainty, altered street access, merchant survey and ad redemptions, conclusions.
**Safety Brief:** qualified review, crossings as exposure denominator, conflict candidate definitions, false positive ratio, restricted observation notes, options requiring study, no blame.
**REVELV Campaign:** eligible triggers, actually served placements, valid showings, verified viewer interactions, conversions externally confirmed if any, voids/discrepancies.
**Community:** metric definitions, access and concern submissions, proposed projects, funded progress only if actual funds received.

## Field data QA checklist
- Strata: daytime/nighttime, quiet/peak, weekday/weekend, rain, motorcycles, crowd occlusion, vehicle headlights.
- At least two independent annotators adjudicate subset; keep validation report and sample version.
- Compute per-class and per-zone errors; set acceptable tolerance *before collecting acceptance set*.
- Confirm object counting does not accidentally double count U-turns or stops across zone lines.
- Simulate missing camera feed; report as unavailable, not zero.
- Evaluate any privacy blur/delay effect on stream and clip workflow.

## Minimum equipment categories (do not purchase before survey)
- Licensed weather-rated camera + mount
- Network router/backhaul + secure credentials
- Edge compute candidate (NVIDIA optional) with power/thermal headroom
- UPS/power protection
- Encrypted local buffer and remote operations channel
- Appropriate signs and public info
- Maintenance access
Compare with manual survey or existing aggregate sensors in procurement.

## Stakeholder interview questions
1. What decision do you make today from guesswork?
2. What would a reliable weekly count enable you to do differently?
3. Are you willing to pay for that information? Under what proof?
4. What would worry you about the public-facing measurement?
5. Would you prefer a report, API, alert, poster, social segment or interactive map?
6. What is an existing event/lighting/wayfinding issue to evaluate?
7. Which datasets can you legitimately share to validate outcomes?

## Go/no-go rubric
- G1 signed permissions and community notice passed.
- G4 measured class/lighting quality passed.
- G5 operator usability and system access review passed.
- G6 stakeholder decision proof passed.
- G7 four-show audience learning completed; media may be paused independently.
- G8 sponsor paid pilot and ledger tested; if not, continue B2B utility before advertising scale.
- G9 civic publication reviewed.

## Expansion to Puerto Plata
Historic-district pilot adds **heritage and AR approvals**; do not assume literal physical projections. Use one voluntarily participating facade and phone-based AR first. Assess visitor wayfinding and local gallery/cafe QR activation.

## Expansion to Hackensack
Reassess permits, NJ/US regulations, insurance, agreements, privacy and site geometry from scratch. Test transferability of daypart metrics, localization, merchant needs, model accuracy and media tone before claiming multi-city repeatability.
