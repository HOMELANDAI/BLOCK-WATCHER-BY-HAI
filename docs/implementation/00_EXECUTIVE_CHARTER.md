# BLOCK WATCH by HAI — Executive Project Charter
**Status:** Implementation-planning baseline, not evidence of a deployed system.
**Product:** Civic Intelligence Operating System
**Sponsor/brand:** HOMELANDAI (HAI)
**Primary candidate market:** Pedro Clisante corridor, Sosúa, Dominican Republic
**Secondary market:** Puerto Plata historic central district
**Transferability test:** Main Street, Hackensack, New Jersey

## Mission
Transform under-measured bustling small towns and compact commercial districts into places that can understand activity, communicate their stories, improve services, support local businesses, and make defensible civic decisions.

The project is an integrated *street intelligence + local media + business enablement + civic impact* platform. It is **not** automatically a public-surveillance product, an accident-prediction authority, or a guaranteed way to increase business revenue.

## Product Architecture: Four Lenses, One Data Backbone
1. **BLOCK WATCH Intelligence** — operational foot-traffic, motorbike, bicycle, vehicle, congestion, event, and carefully reviewed conflict analytics; intelligence for business owners, property owners, planners, tourism partners, and event producers.
2. **BLOCK WATCH Media** — Twitch/YouTube shows, optional Maestro experiences, local NPC characters, forecasts/predictions, meaningful viewer participation, weekly recaps, and Intersection Games.
3. **BLOCK WATCH Civic** — transparent Community Vitality Metrics, public infrastructure evidence, walkability, tourism access, before/after program evaluation, open public summaries, and community feedback.
4. **REVELV + HAI Micro-Ad Lab** — animated creative production, sponsor campaigns, AR-enabled building placements, consent-based interactions, ad-serving records, verified delivery, business reporting, and an optional auditable civic dividend.

**Shared technology:** privacy-conscious computer vision + event collection + measurement governance + Supabase + vendor-independent processing + broadcast, AR, and reporting adapters.

## Market Segmentation
| Type | Situation | First product to sell | Economics |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Quiet small town | Low counts and low event frequency | Periodic surveys, baseline planning, event measurement | Subscription or project-fee analytics; no expensive always-on media assumptions |
| Emerging town | Uneven growth, vacant storefronts, new investments | Event impact, leasing evidence, occupancy, before/after analysis | Economic development and property stakeholders |
| Bustling small town/city | Dense activity in compact corridors, tourism/nightlife/events | Operating intelligence + sponsors + media + civic insights | Strongest starting candidate; one deployment creates multiple customer value streams |

**Do not select towns by population alone.** Score activity density, spend-capable anchor institutions, street complexity, permissions, tourism/event diversity, stakeholder capacity, business willingness to pay, and camera feasibility.

## Critical Distinctions
- **Direct measures:** counted crossings, directional movement, periods, occupancy samples, QR redemptions, ad deliveries.
- **Derived estimates:** dwell time, path distribution, conflict candidates, return-visit proxy, inferred peaks.
- **External-confirmed measures:** sales, hotel occupancy, bookings, public safety outcomes, rents, tax revenue.
- **Predictions:** forecasting outputs with uncertainty bands and documented backtesting.
- **Causal effects:** require appropriate controlled design; a change in pedestrian volume is not proof HAI caused business growth.

Never describe a camera count as unique visitors, an impression as a person, a near-miss candidate as an accident, or an engagement as a sale.

## Customers and Their Decisions
- **Restaurant/bar:** staffing, promotion windows, inventory, closing hours, event nights.
- **Boutique/retail:** hours, merchandising, promotion, local corridor partnerships.
- **Property owner/leasing agent:** leasing pitches and site comparisons using normalized exposure metrics.
- **Tourism office:** visitor routing, dead zones, event dispersal, wayfinding opportunities.
- **Event organizer:** entry/exit patterns, audience distribution, vendor placement, congestion, sponsor evidence.
- **City/planner:** corridor safety review, lighting priorities, pedestrian phases, transport staging, maintenance.
- **Business organization:** recruitment gap hypotheses and collective marketing.
- **Creator/broadcaster:** local storytelling with audience participation.
- **Residents:** accessible public summaries, civic proposals, governance and visible reinvestment.

## Project Success Model
**Value chain:** Measure → Interpret → Act → Evaluate → Communicate → Monetize responsibly → Return value.
The first independent proof must be that at least one stakeholder makes a better-informed operational decision using reliable metrics. Entertainment and ad revenue are second and third proofs, not prerequisites for intelligence utility.

## Governance Commitments
- Camera rights, property authorization, municipal approvals, signage, and local counsel review before any live public installation.
- Aggregate-first collection; no default face identification, person-level tracking across venues, license-plate recognition, or identifying passersby for ads.
- Safety findings are hypotheses for trained human review; no public naming/shaming.
- Commercial sponsors never influence safety findings or public metric definitions.
- Public reports contain measurement methodology, confidence/data-quality caveats, and aggregation thresholds.
- Civic fund uses written policy, disclosures, audits, and transparent project status.
- Products and platform features are labeled **planned / prototype / tested / production**, never implied live before verification.

## Non-goals for MVP
- Comprehensive citywide surveillance
- Automated traffic enforcement
- Predicting an individual pedestrian's conduct
- Automatically diagnosing crashes
- Raw 24/7 unfiltered public camera broadcast
- Identity-based ad triggers using street cameras
- Citywide 3D reconstruction
- Guaranteed sponsor ROI
- Automatically changing municipal infrastructure

## High-level schedule (planning hypothesis)
Month numbers are relative to **kickoff**, not promised calendar dates:
- M0–M2: Phase 0 market validation, stakeholder discovery, site feasibility
- M1–M3: Phase 1 privacy and measurement governance, permits, pilot design
- M2–M5: Phase 2 data platform and recorded-video simulation
- M3–M6: Phase 3 CV pilot and measurement quality gates
- M4–M7: Phase 4 operations dashboard and reporting MVP
- M5–M8: Phase 5 business/tourism/event intelligence pilot
- M6–M9: Phase 6 NPC broadcast and viewer tests
- M7–M10: Phase 7 REVELV and Micro-Ad Lab pilot
- M8–M12: Phase 8 civic/safety evaluation and public reporting
- M10–M14: Phase 9 spatial ads, AR and digital-twin feasibility
- M12–M18: Phase 10 multi-corridor / second-city repeatability

These may run concurrently *only when their safety and dependency gates are met*. Actual duration depends heavily on permissions, staff, hardware, and access.

## Definition of Project Done
BLOCK WATCH is a pilot-ready product only when counting accuracy is independently reviewed, data access is secured, permissions documented, operator procedures validated, end-to-end reporting demonstrated, and at least one legitimate stakeholder use case is measured. A fully realized multi-city product requires repeatable deployments and verified unit economics, not merely polished visuals.
